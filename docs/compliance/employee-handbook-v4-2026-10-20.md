PURPOSE AND STATUS OF THIS HANDBOOK

This Handbook describes the principal HR policies and procedures that apply to all employees of Champ Fitness Ltd (the "Company"). It is incorporated by reference into your contract of employment but is itself non-contractual — the Company may amend, replace, or withdraw any policy in this Handbook from time to time and will give you reasonable notice of material changes. Where this Handbook conflicts with your written contract of employment, your contract prevails.

In this Handbook, "you" and "your" mean the employee; "we", "us", "our", and "the Company" mean the Employer.

1. CODE OF CONDUCT AND STANDARDS

You are expected to:

- Treat colleagues, members, contractors, and visitors with respect and courtesy at all times.
- Arrive on time for your scheduled shifts, in the agreed uniform, and ready to work.
- Follow lawful instructions from your manager and the Company's published procedures.
- Maintain the confidentiality of member, business, and colleague information.
- Behave honestly and with integrity in all dealings on behalf of the Company.
- Promptly report any conduct, condition, or circumstance that may cause harm or that contravenes this Handbook.

Conduct that the Company considers gross misconduct may lead to summary dismissal (dismissal without notice or pay in lieu). The list below is not exhaustive:

- Theft, fraud, deliberate falsification of records, or dishonest claiming of expenses.
- Physical violence, threats of violence, or serious verbal abuse.
- Sexual harassment, bullying, or discrimination in breach of Section 10.
- Wilful or grossly negligent damage to Company or member property.
- Breach of confidentiality or data protection (Section 11 of this Handbook and the Staff Privacy Notice).
- Working while under the influence of alcohol or unlawful drugs (Section 13).
- Serious breach of health and safety rules, including any deliberate act that endangers a colleague, member, or visitor.
- Conviction of a criminal offence that is incompatible with the role.


2. HOURS, BREAKS, AND THE RIGHT TO DISCONNECT

2.1 Your contracted hours, working pattern, and any shift premia are set out in your contract of employment.

2.2 We comply with the Organisation of Working Time Act 1997 ("OWTA"):

- Maximum average weekly working hours: 48 hours, averaged over a 4-month reference period.
- Daily rest: 11 consecutive hours in each 24-hour period.
- Weekly rest: 24 consecutive hours in each 7-day period, normally including Sunday.
- Breaks: 15 minutes after 4.5 hours of work; 30 minutes (which may include the 15) after 6 hours.

2.3 You are required to record your working time (including breaks) accurately in the rostering system. Failure to record working time correctly may be treated as a disciplinary matter.

2.4 Right to Disconnect. In line with the WRC Code of Practice on the Right to Disconnect:

- You are not required to routinely perform work outside your normal working hours.
- You will not be penalised for not responding to non-urgent emails, WhatsApp messages, or other communications outside your normal working hours.
- You are expected to respect the right of colleagues to disconnect — avoid sending non-urgent messages to colleagues outside their working hours where possible, or use the platform's "schedule send" feature.
- A small number of roles (incident response, member-safety on-call) may require occasional out-of-hours availability — those expectations are documented in writing and compensated separately.

2.5 Automatic attendance recording. Where the Company has enabled it at your location, the Repset staff app records the time you arrive at the gym and matches it to your rostered shift. You do not need to clock in manually for that shift start.

2.6 The app detects arrival at the workplace only. It identifies that you have arrived at the gym premises; it does not record where you are at any other time, and it does not track your movements between locations or outside your working hours. For this to work, location access on your phone must be set to "Always". The app will ask you for this permission and will not open until it is granted.

2.7 Automatic detection supports, but does not replace, your obligation under 2.3 to record working time accurately. If detection fails — your phone is off, out of battery, left at home, or the arrival simply does not register — tell your manager and your attendance will be recorded manually. You will not be treated as absent or late, or as being in breach of 2.3, solely because a device did not report.

2.8 Opting out. If you would prefer not to use automatic detection, or you do not carry a phone suitable for it, tell your manager. You will be exempted and your attendance recorded manually instead. Being exempt has no effect on your terms of employment or your standing with the Company. Attendance data is used for payroll, rostering, and the management of working time only — the Staff Privacy Notice sets out how it is handled and how you can object.


3. ANNUAL LEAVE AND PUBLIC HOLIDAYS

3.1 The leave year runs from 1 January to 31 December.

3.2 Full-time employees are entitled to 20 working days of paid annual leave per leave year (the statutory minimum under OWTA). Pro-rata for part-time staff. Your contract may grant more — the contract prevails where it is more generous.

3.3 Booking leave:

- Submit requests through the scheduling system at least 4 weeks in advance for blocks of one week or more, and 7 days in advance for shorter requests.
- Approval is subject to operational requirements; we will not unreasonably refuse a request, but coverage at peak class times takes priority.
- A maximum of 5 days may be carried into the following leave year, and must be used by 31 March. The Company may pay accrued unused leave in lieu only on termination.

3.4 Public holidays. Ireland has nine public holidays per year. If a public holiday falls on a day you would normally have worked, you receive one of: a paid day off on the day; a paid day off within a month; an additional day's annual leave; or an additional day's pay — at the Company's reasonable choice and communicated in advance. If you have not worked at least 40 hours in the 5 weeks immediately preceding the public holiday, you have no entitlement for that day.

3.5 Sickness during annual leave. If you become genuinely ill during pre-booked annual leave and provide a medical certificate covering the relevant days, those days will be reclassified as sick leave and re-credited to your annual leave balance.


4. SICK LEAVE AND THE STATUTORY SICK PAY SCHEME

4.1 Notification. If you cannot attend work due to illness or injury, you must:

- Notify your manager or duty manager by phone (not text or message) as early as possible before your shift starts.
- Provide an indication of how long you expect to be absent.
- Keep us updated daily if your absence extends, until you obtain a medical certificate.

4.2 Medical certificates. A certificate from a registered medical practitioner is required:

- From the first day of absence if you wish to claim Statutory Sick Pay or any company sick pay.
- For any absence of more than 2 consecutive working days.
- For any absence on the day immediately before or after a weekend, public holiday, or annual leave.
- On request from your manager.

4.3 Statutory Sick Pay (SSP). You are entitled to SSP under the Sick Leave Act 2022, subject to:

- Having completed 13 weeks' continuous service with the Company.
- Providing a medical certificate from a registered medical practitioner.
- Not being entitled to a more favourable Company sick-pay scheme for the same days.

SSP is paid at the statutory daily rate (currently 70% of your normal daily wages, capped at the statutory daily maximum) for the statutory number of certified days per calendar year, as in force from time to time. The Company applies the SSP rules as they apply on the day of absence.

4.4 Company sick pay. The Company does not currently operate a discretionary sick-pay scheme in addition to Statutory Sick Pay.

4.5 Long-term illness. If you are absent for more than 4 weeks continuously, we may, with your consent, arrange a referral to an occupational health practitioner. We may also discuss your fitness to return to work and any reasonable accommodations under the Employment Equality Acts.

4.6 Return to work. On return from any absence of 3 or more working days, your manager will conduct a brief return-to-work meeting to confirm fitness, identify any support you need, and discuss patterns of absence where relevant. This is not a disciplinary meeting.

4.7 Unauthorised absence. Absence without notification, or absence not supported by a medical certificate where one is required, may be treated as unauthorised and may lead to disciplinary action. Pay is not due for unauthorised absence.


5. OTHER STATUTORY LEAVE ENTITLEMENTS

You may be entitled to the following statutory leave on the conditions set out in the relevant legislation. Speak to your manager or HR for full details and to submit a request.

- Maternity leave — 26 weeks (Maternity Protection Acts 1994 and 2004), plus up to 16 weeks unpaid.
- Paternity leave — 2 weeks (Paternity Leave and Benefit Act 2016).
- Parent's leave — 9 weeks within the child's first 2 years (Parent's Leave and Benefit Act 2019, as amended).
- Adoptive leave — 24 weeks (Adoptive Leave Acts 1995 and 2005), plus up to 16 weeks unpaid.
- Parental leave — up to 26 weeks per child to age 12 (Parental Leave Acts 1998 and 2019).
- Carer's leave — up to 104 weeks (Carer's Leave Act 2001).
- Force majeure leave — up to 3 days in any 12-month period, or 5 days in 36 months (Parental Leave Act 1998).
- Medical care leave — up to 5 days unpaid per 12-month period for serious medical need of a relevant person (Work Life Balance and Miscellaneous Provisions Act 2023).
- Leave for victims of domestic violence — up to 10 days paid (Work Life Balance and Miscellaneous Provisions Act 2023). All requests treated in the strictest confidence.
- Jury service — paid time off as required (Juries Act 1976).

Statutory benefits (Maternity Benefit, Paternity Benefit, etc.) are paid by the Department of Social Protection — separate from any Company top-up.


6. PROBATIONARY PERIOD

6.1 Unless your contract states otherwise, your first 6 months of employment are a probationary period during which we assess your suitability for continued employment. The Company may extend the probationary period by up to a further 3 months where reasonable to do so, in accordance with the Probationary Periods (Employee Information) Act 2022. The total probationary period (including any extension) will not exceed 12 months.

6.2 During probation, your manager will conduct check-ins at approximately 1 month, 3 months, and end of probation, covering performance, fit, and any support you need.

6.3 During probation, either party may terminate this Agreement by giving the statutory minimum notice required by the Minimum Notice and Terms of Employment Act 1973 (one week from either side during the first 13 weeks of service).

6.4 Successful completion of probation will be confirmed to you in writing. We do not automatically confirm employment on the lapse of probation — silence does not mean confirmation; ask your manager if you have not received written confirmation by the end of your probationary period.


7. PERFORMANCE, DEVELOPMENT, AND REVIEWS

7.1 We hold a structured performance review with each employee at least annually, in addition to ongoing informal feedback. The annual review covers:

- Performance against agreed objectives or KPIs.
- Member feedback (where relevant to a member-facing role).
- Certifications and continuing-professional-development progress.
- Development goals and training needs for the coming period.
- Any reasonable adjustments or working-arrangement requests.

7.2 Where performance falls below the standards expected, your manager will raise the issue informally first and provide reasonable opportunity, support, and time to improve. Persistent or serious under-performance not resolved informally will be addressed under the Disciplinary Procedure (Section 8).


8. DISCIPLINARY PROCEDURE

This procedure is based on the SI 146/2000 Code of Practice on Grievance and Disciplinary Procedures.

8.1 Informal stage. Most minor issues are dealt with informally between you and your manager — a private conversation explaining the concern, what good performance or conduct looks like, and any support available. No formal record is kept in your HR file, but your manager may keep a contemporaneous note.

8.2 Right to representation. At any formal stage, you have the right to be accompanied by a colleague or trade union representative. Notify the chair of the meeting in advance of who will accompany you.

8.3 Investigation. If informal resolution is not appropriate or has not succeeded, your manager (or a manager nominated by the Company) will conduct an investigation: gathering evidence, interviewing relevant witnesses, and giving you the opportunity to give your account. Investigation is not itself a disciplinary stage; it determines whether there is a case to answer.

8.4 Suspension during investigation. In serious cases the Company may place you on paid suspension while the investigation is conducted. Suspension is a neutral act and not a sanction.

8.5 Formal stages. Where a case to answer exists, one of the following formal stages will apply, depending on severity:

- Stage 1 — Verbal warning. For minor issues. Lives on your file for 6 months from the date issued; spent thereafter.
- Stage 2 — Written warning. For more serious issues or repeated minor issues. Lives on your file for 9 months.
- Stage 3 — Final written warning. For serious issues or further repetition. Lives on your file for 12 months.
- Stage 4 — Dismissal. For very serious issues, gross misconduct, or failure to improve after a final written warning. Dismissal for gross misconduct may be summary (without notice). Dismissal for other reasons will be on contractual notice or pay in lieu.

You may be entered at any stage depending on the severity of the matter; the procedure is not necessarily sequential.

8.6 Disciplinary meetings. You will be:

- Notified in writing of the matter to be discussed and given reasonable time to prepare (normally at least 3 working days).
- Provided with any documents that will be relied upon at the meeting.
- Heard at a meeting chaired by a manager not previously involved in the investigation (where reasonably possible).
- Notified of the outcome in writing, with reasons.

8.7 Appeal. You may appeal any formal sanction within 5 working days of receiving the written outcome. Send the appeal in writing to the next-most-senior manager not previously involved (or, where that is the Owner, to the Owner, Richard Ivers — richard@richardivers.com) stating the grounds of appeal. The appeal will be heard within a reasonable period by a manager not previously involved in the matter. The appeal outcome is final within the Company.

8.8 Statutory rights. Nothing in this procedure removes your right, where applicable, to refer a complaint of unfair dismissal to the Workplace Relations Commission within 6 months of dismissal (extendable to 12 months with reasonable cause).


9. GRIEVANCE PROCEDURE

9.1 A grievance is any concern, problem, or complaint you have about your work, working conditions, or relationships with colleagues, managers, members, or the Company. This procedure does not apply to grievances about pay (which go directly to payroll), allegations of bullying or harassment (which are dealt with under Section 10), or protected disclosures (which go through Section 11).

9.2 Informal stage. Raise the matter with your line manager as soon as possible. The manager will discuss the matter with you and aim for an informal resolution within a reasonable timeframe.

9.3 Formal stage 1. If informal resolution is not possible or appropriate, put your grievance in writing to your line manager (or, if the grievance concerns the manager, to that manager's manager). Include:

- A clear description of the issue.
- When and where it occurred.
- Anyone else involved or who witnessed it.
- The outcome you are seeking.

9.4 The recipient will arrange a grievance meeting within 10 working days of receiving the written grievance. You may be accompanied by a colleague or trade union representative. Following the meeting, the recipient may conduct further enquiries, and will provide a written outcome within a reasonable period (normally 10 working days from the meeting).

9.5 Appeal. If you are not satisfied with the outcome, you may appeal in writing within 5 working days, stating the grounds of appeal. The appeal will be heard by a manager more senior than, and not previously involved with, the original decision-maker.

9.6 Confidentiality. Both you and the Company will treat the grievance, the meetings, and the outcomes confidentially. We will not victimise you for raising a grievance in good faith.

9.7 Statutory rights. Nothing in this procedure removes your right to refer a complaint to the Workplace Relations Commission.


10. DIGNITY AT WORK — BULLYING, HARASSMENT, AND EQUAL OPPORTUNITIES

10.1 The Company is committed to providing a working environment free from bullying, harassment, sexual harassment, victimisation, and discrimination. This commitment applies to relationships between colleagues, with members, and with contractors and visitors.

10.2 Discrimination. We comply with the Employment Equality Acts 1998–2015. Discrimination on any of the nine protected grounds is prohibited:

- Gender — including transgender identity.
- Civil status.
- Family status.
- Sexual orientation.
- Religion.
- Age (does not apply to anyone under 18 for employment purposes).
- Disability.
- Race — including colour, nationality, and ethnic or national origin.
- Membership of the Traveller community.

10.3 Bullying. Bullying is repeated inappropriate behaviour, direct or indirect, by a person against another, which could reasonably be regarded as undermining the individual's right to dignity at work. A single incident may, depending on severity, also constitute bullying. We follow the Code of Practice for Employers and Employees on the Prevention and Resolution of Bullying at Work (2021).

10.4 Sexual harassment. Sexual harassment is any form of unwanted verbal, non-verbal, or physical conduct of a sexual nature that has the purpose or effect of violating a person's dignity, in particular by creating an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating, or offensive environment. A single incident may amount to sexual harassment.

10.5 Reporting. If you experience or witness bullying, harassment, sexual harassment, or discrimination:

- Where you feel safe and able to do so, raise the matter directly with the person concerned and ask them to stop.
- Otherwise, or if the behaviour continues, raise it with your manager. If the matter involves your manager, raise it with the Owner directly — Richard Ivers (Owner) — richard@richardivers.com.

10.6 The Company will investigate complaints promptly, sensitively, and confidentially. We will not victimise you for raising a complaint in good faith. Findings of bullying, harassment, or discrimination will be addressed under the Disciplinary Procedure. Where the finding is of gross misconduct, this may lead to summary dismissal.

10.7 Reasonable accommodations. If you have a disability, you may request reasonable accommodations to enable you to perform your role. Submit the request in writing to your manager or HR. The Company will consider any request and, where the accommodation is reasonable having regard to its cost, impact on the business, and your specific needs, will implement it.


11. PROTECTED DISCLOSURES (WHISTLEBLOWING)

11.1 The Company encourages you to raise concerns about wrongdoing in or by the organisation without fear of reprisal. This procedure operates in addition to and alongside the Grievance Procedure (which is for personal employment-related complaints).

11.2 A "relevant wrongdoing" under the Protected Disclosures Act 2014 (as amended) includes:

- That an offence has been, is being, or is likely to be committed.
- That a person has failed, is failing, or is likely to fail to comply with a legal obligation.
- That a miscarriage of justice has occurred, is occurring, or is likely to occur.
- That the health or safety of any individual has been, is being, or is likely to be endangered.
- That the environment has been, is being, or is likely to be damaged.
- That an unlawful or otherwise improper use of public funds has occurred.
- That an act or omission by or on behalf of a public body is oppressive, discriminatory, or grossly negligent.
- That information tending to show any of the above has been, is being, or is likely to be concealed or destroyed.
- A breach of any EU law within scope of the Act.

11.3 How to make a disclosure. You may make a disclosure:

- Internally — to your manager, to the Designated Protected Disclosures Officer, Richard Ivers (Owner) — richard@richardivers.com, or in writing to Champ Fitness Ltd, 1st Floor, Stillorgan Village Centre, Stillorgan, Dublin, A94 AC67.
- Externally — to the prescribed person for the relevant area, or to the Office of the Protected Disclosures Commissioner, in accordance with the Act.

11.4 We will:

- Acknowledge your disclosure in writing within 7 days.
- Diligently follow up on the matter, within a reasonable time and not later than 3 months from acknowledgement.
- Communicate the outcome to you in writing.
- Maintain confidentiality of your identity to the extent possible, except where disclosure is necessary by law.

11.5 Protection. We will not penalise, dismiss, or otherwise disadvantage you for making a protected disclosure in good faith. Acts of penalisation by another employee may themselves be a disciplinary matter under Section 8.


12. REMOTE AND HYBRID WORKING

12.1 Where the nature of your role permits remote or hybrid working, you may submit a request under the Work Life Balance and Miscellaneous Provisions Act 2023. Submit the request in writing to your manager at least 8 weeks before the requested start date, including:

- The proposed arrangement and start date.
- The reasons for the request.
- Information about the proposed remote location and the suitability of that location (workspace, connectivity, confidentiality).

12.2 The Company will consider your request reasonably, having regard to operational requirements, role suitability, the cost of facilitating remote work, and any health and safety implications. We will respond in writing within 4 weeks (extendable by a further 8 weeks where required) with a decision and reasons.

12.3 If remote or hybrid working is approved, you will:

- Keep agreed working hours and remain available during those hours.
- Use Company devices and accounts for all work, in accordance with the Acceptable Use Policy.
- Take reasonable care for the safety and suitability of your remote workspace and complete any home-working risk assessment requested by the Company.
- Keep personal and member data confidential and not work in any location where the data could be overlooked by third parties.
- Attend the studio in person on agreed days and for specific events (team meetings, training, member events) on reasonable notice.

12.4 The Company may withdraw an approved remote or hybrid arrangement on reasonable notice where the operational, performance, or compliance basis on which it was granted no longer holds.


13. DRUGS, ALCOHOL, AND INTOXICANTS

13.1 You must not attend work, or remain at work, while under the influence of alcohol, unlawful drugs, or any other intoxicant that may impair your judgement, performance, or the safety of others. This is a safety-critical rule for a fitness business — coaching attendees, supervising weights, and operating premises require full faculties.

13.2 The Company does not permit consumption of alcohol or intoxicants on the premises during working hours, except at sanctioned Company events where moderate consumption is expressly authorised by the Company.

13.3 Prescription and over-the-counter medication. If you are taking prescribed or over-the-counter medication that may affect your performance or safety at work, inform your manager so that reasonable adjustments can be made.

13.4 Where you reasonably believe a colleague is under the influence at work, raise the concern with your manager immediately. Working under the influence is treated as gross misconduct.

13.5 The Company may, where there is reasonable cause to believe you are under the influence and the safety of others is at risk, ask you to stop work and leave the premises pending an investigation under the Disciplinary Procedure.

13.6 Substance misuse. We recognise that substance misuse can be a health issue. Where you voluntarily disclose a problem before any disciplinary process has commenced, we will, in our discretion, work with you to access appropriate support.


14. HEALTH, SAFETY, AND WELFARE

14.1 The Safety, Health and Welfare at Work Act 2005 imposes duties on both the Company and on you. The Company's safety statement is available from your manager and in the staff area, and is reviewed at least annually.

14.2 Your duties under the Act include:

- Taking reasonable care for your own safety, health, and welfare and that of any other person who may be affected by your acts or omissions.
- Co-operating with the Company on safety matters.
- Not engaging in any improper conduct that could endanger anyone.
- Using personal protective equipment provided to you, where required.
- Attending and undergoing any reasonable health and safety training provided.
- Reporting any defects in equipment, the workplace, or systems of work without unreasonable delay.
- Reporting any accident, dangerous occurrence, or near-miss to your manager as soon as possible.

14.3 Manual handling. You must complete the Company's manual-handling training before lifting, carrying, or moving heavy equipment, weights, or members (where applicable). Refresher training is required every 3 years.

14.4 First aid. The names of trained first-aiders and the locations of first-aid kits and AEDs are displayed on the staff noticeboard and on the staff intranet. In a member-safety incident, follow the published emergency procedure.

14.5 Accident reporting. All accidents, dangerous occurrences, and near-misses must be reported in the accident book within 24 hours. Serious incidents (those resulting in hospital treatment, or any incident reportable under the Safety, Health and Welfare at Work (Reporting of Accidents and Dangerous Occurrences) Regulations 2016) must also be reported to your manager immediately.

14.6 Fitness for work. If you have a medical condition that may affect your ability to perform your duties safely, you must inform the Company. We will not unfairly disadvantage you for doing so; we may request medical evidence and consider reasonable accommodations.


15. MEMBER CARE, PROFESSIONAL CONDUCT, AND CERTIFICATIONS

15.1 Member-facing professionalism. You represent the Company in every interaction with members and prospects. You will:

- Greet members by name where possible and treat each member with respect.
- Provide instruction, advice, and supervision in line with your training and certifications, your role description, and the Company's safety SOPs.
- Not give medical, dietetic, or therapeutic advice beyond the scope of your qualifications. Refer members to a qualified professional where appropriate.
- Not enter into personal financial transactions with members (lending, borrowing, personal training arrangements outside the Company, sale of supplements or services not authorised by the Company).
- Not develop romantic or sexual relationships with members in your direct coaching responsibility, and disclose any pre-existing relationship to your manager so that conflicts can be managed.

15.2 Certifications. Where your role requires professional certifications (personal training, group instructor, first aid, CPR, manual handling), you are responsible for:

- Maintaining them in good standing throughout your employment.
- Providing evidence of current certification on commencement and within 14 days of each renewal.
- Notifying the Company immediately of any disciplinary process, complaint, or investigation in relation to a certification or governing body.
- Bearing the cost of renewals at your own expense, unless otherwise agreed in writing.

15.3 Where any required certification lapses, is revoked, or cannot be evidenced, the Company may suspend you without pay or terminate your contract.

15.4 Class delivery and programming. Class plans, training programmes, and member-facing materials must be approved by your manager or the head coach. You may not introduce new movements, exercises, or programming variations into a class without sign-off. All members' injury history and medical disclosures must be checked before class start and adjustments made accordingly.

15.5 Member confidentiality. See the Staff Privacy Notice and clauses 36–37 of your employment contract. Disclosing member health data, training data, or personal contact details to anyone inside or outside the Company beyond the scope of your duties is gross misconduct.


16. USE OF COMPANY PROPERTY AND RETURN ON TERMINATION

16.1 Property issued to you (uniform, keys, fobs, lockers, devices, equipment) remains the property of the Company and is to be used only for your role. Take reasonable care and return it on request or on termination of employment in good condition (fair wear and tear excepted).

16.2 Property to be returned on or before your last working day includes (where applicable): keys and fobs; uniform; any Company-issued mobile device, laptop, tablet, or other equipment; identification card; documents and notebooks containing Company or member information; and any other Company property held by you.

16.3 By signing your contract, you have authorised the Company to deduct from your final pay the cost of replacing any property not returned, or returned damaged beyond fair wear and tear (see clause 13 of your contract — Authorised Deductions).

16.4 You are not permitted to retain copies of Company or member documents, records, presentations, photos, videos, or data on personal devices, accounts, or cloud storage after termination. See the Acceptable Use Policy for monitoring rights and the data-deletion procedure on exit.


17. SOCIAL MEDIA AND EXTERNAL COMMUNICATIONS

- Make it clear when posting in a personal capacity vs as a representative of the Company.
- Do not disclose confidential or member information.
- Do not post photos or videos featuring members without consent.
- Do not engage in commentary that brings the Company or its members into disrepute.
- Refer media enquiries about the Company to the Owner, Richard Ivers — richard@richardivers.com.

17.2 During and after employment, do not use the Company's trade marks, logos, or brand-protected imagery without written permission.
